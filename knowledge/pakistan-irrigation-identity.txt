# Pakistan Indus Basin irrigation and NADRA digital identity
# One fact per line, phrased with the words someone would ask with.

The Indus Basin Irrigation System, IBIS, is the largest contiguous irrigation network in the world and the backbone of Pakistan's agriculture, diverting water from the Indus and its tributaries.
Tarbela reservoir on the Indus and Mangla reservoir on the Jhelum are Pakistan's primary storage hubs, capturing glacial melt and monsoon runoff and stabilising flow for the canal network.
Tarbela and Mangla dams are also Pakistan's main sources of hydroelectric power, supplying thousands of megawatts at peak summer demand.
The Sukkur, Kotri and Taunsa barrages divert river water into Pakistan's irrigation canals and protect downstream areas from seasonal floods.
The Sukkur Barrage alone controls seven primary canals irrigating millions of acres in the Sindh plains by gravity-fed distribution.
Link canals such as the Chashma-Jhelum Link Canal transfer water from the western rivers, the Indus and Jhelum, to the eastern rivers, the Chenab, Ravi and Sutlej, to balance supply.

NADRA, the National Database and Registration Authority, runs Pakistan's centralised biometric citizen identification system.
NADRA's National Data Repository holds unique identities for over 120 million citizens, using automated fingerprint identification, AFIS, together with facial recognition.
The Smart National Identity Card, SNIC, embeds a tamper-proof microchip holding encrypted citizen data, dual fingerprint templates and a unique 13-digit identity number mapped across family trees.
Verisys is NADRA's real-time identity verification gateway, used by telecom operators for biometric SIM activation, by banks for KYC compliance, and by the Raast payment system.
