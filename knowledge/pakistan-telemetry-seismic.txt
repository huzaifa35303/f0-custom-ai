# Pakistan motorway telemetry and seismic monitoring
# One fact per line, phrased with the words someone would ask with.

Pakistan's National Highways and Motorway Police, NHMP, run traffic monitoring, automated tolling and security on the M-1, M-2, M-3, M-4, M-5, M-9 and M-11 motorways.
Pakistan's E-Tag electronic toll collection uses RFID transponders on the M-2 and M-9 gates, integrated with the OneNetwork and M-Tag systems so vehicles pay without stopping.
ANPR automatic number plate recognition cameras at Pakistani motorway interchanges and toll booths check vehicle registrations against national crime databases in real time.
Pakistan's motorways use point-to-point speed cameras and weigh-in-motion, WIM, sensors embedded in commercial lanes to catch overloaded freight trucks.
An underground fibre-optic backbone runs the length of Pakistan's major motorways, connecting intelligent transport system sensors to regional command rooms.

The Pakistan Meteorological Department, PMD, monitors earthquakes where the Indian, Eurasian and Arabian plates collide.
The National Seismic Monitoring Centre, NSMC, is headquartered in Karachi with backup processing in Islamabad, receiving live telemetry from broadband stations by satellite and radio.
The Chaman fault array monitors a strike-slip transform fault across Balochistan using geodetic GPS arrays to measure crustal stress.
The Main Karakoram Thrust, MKT, monitoring network covers Gilgit-Baltistan and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, tracking compression from the northward push of the Indian plate.
The Makran tsunami early warning network sits over the subduction zone off Pakistan's southern coast, using tide gauges and ocean floor pressure sensors to alert Karachi, Gwadar and Pasni.
