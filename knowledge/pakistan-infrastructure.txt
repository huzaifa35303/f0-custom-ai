# Pakistan Infrastructure, Logistics and Grid
# One fact per line, phrased with the words someone would ask with.

The Matiari-Lahore HVDC line is a 660 kV direct current transmission line of 4,000 MW capacity and about 886 km, carrying power from southern coal and nuclear plants to northern grids.
The CASA-1000 project is a 500 kV cross-border link designed to import 1,300 MW of hydropower from Kyrgyzstan and Tajikistan through Afghanistan into Pakistan at Nowshera.
The Neelum-Jhelum transmission link is a 500 kV double-circuit line carrying 969 MW of run-of-the-river hydro power into the national pool.

Port Muhammad Bin Qasim has a 13-metre navigable channel draught and more than 12 deep-water berths.
Port Qasim handles over 35 percent of Pakistan's national cargo volume, especially bulk industrial inputs, LNG, raw minerals and heavy machinery.
Karachi Port Trust, KPT, is a natural deep-water harbour with draught limits of 11.5 to 13 metres and is historically Pakistan's busiest port.
Gwadar deep-sea port has a design capability up to 14.5 metres and is the main maritime node of CPEC, bypassing the Malacca Strait.
Gwadar is described as the world's largest deep-sea warm-water port, at the mouth of the Persian Gulf, giving landlocked Central Asia a direct energy route.

Pakistan Security Printing Corporation, PSPC, is the sole authority for minting banknotes, passports and other high-security state documents, from a secure plant in Karachi.
The State Bank of Pakistan manages foreign exchange reserves across USD, EUR and RMB plus gold, to maintain stability, service sovereign debt and defend import cover.

The Karakoram Highway, KKH, is often called the eighth wonder of the world and cuts through the collision zone of the Eurasian and Indian tectonic plates, linking Pakistan to China at Khunjerab Pass.
Khewra Salt Mine is the world's second largest salt mine, found when Alexander the Great's army horses licked the rocks in 326 BC, and it produces pink Himalayan salt.
