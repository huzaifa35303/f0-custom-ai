# Pakistan UNESCO World Heritage sites and Nishan-e-Haider recipients
# One fact per line, phrased with the words someone would ask with.

Mohenjo-daro became a UNESCO World Heritage site in Pakistan in 1980 and is one of the world's earliest major cities, from the Indus Valley Civilization, known for baked brick buildings and advanced drainage.
Taxila became a UNESCO World Heritage site in Pakistan in 1980 and is an archaeological site on the ancient Silk Road blending Persian, Greek and Buddhist art.
Takht-i-Bahi and Sahr-i-Bahlol became a UNESCO World Heritage site in Pakistan in 1980 and is a well-preserved Buddhist monastic complex from the 1st century CE in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.
Lahore Fort and Shalamar Gardens became a UNESCO World Heritage site in Pakistan in 1981 and are masterpieces of the Mughal era, with marble palaces and Persian paradise gardens.
The historical monuments at Makli in Thatta became a UNESCO World Heritage site in Pakistan in 1981 and form one of the world's largest funerary sites, with about half a million tombs.
Rohtas Fort became a UNESCO World Heritage site in Pakistan in 1997 and is a 16th-century military garrison built by Sher Shah Suri.

The Nishan-e-Haider is Pakistan's highest military gallantry award.
Captain Raja Muhammad Sarwar of the Punjab Regiment was the first recipient of the Nishan-e-Haider, in the Kashmir war of 1948, for breaching enemy barbed wire under machine-gun fire.
Naik Saif Ali Janjua of the Azad Kashmir Regiment was awarded the Hilal-e-Kashmir, equivalent to the Nishan-e-Haider, in the Kashmir war of 1948 for defending a vital post.
Major Tufail Muhammad of the Punjab Regiment received the Nishan-e-Haider for the Lakshmipur border action of 1958, directing operations despite mortal wounds.
Major Raja Aziz Bhatti of the Punjab Regiment received the Nishan-e-Haider in the 1965 Indo-Pak war for holding the BRB canal under continuous shelling.
Pilot Officer Rashid Minhas of the Pakistan Air Force received the Nishan-e-Haider in the 1971 war and is the only PAF recipient, having crashed his hijacked trainer jet to prevent defection.
Major Muhammad Akram of the Frontier Force received the Nishan-e-Haider for the Battle of Hilli in 1971, blocking an enemy brigade's advance.
Major Shabbir Sharif of the Frontier Force received the Nishan-e-Haider in the 1971 war for capturing a bridgehead and destroying several tanks.
Sawar Muhammad Hussain of the Armoured Corps received the Nishan-e-Haider for the Battle of Zafarwal in 1971, guiding anti-tank crews on foot.
Lance Naik Muhammad Mahfuz of the Punjab Regiment received the Nishan-e-Haider in the Wagah-Attari sector in 1971, advancing unarmed on an enemy bunker.
Captain Karnal Sher Khan of the Sindh Regiment and Northern Light Infantry received the Nishan-e-Haider in the Kargil conflict of 1999 for holding posts at 18,400 feet.
Havildar Lalak Jan of the Northern Light Infantry received the Nishan-e-Haider in the Kargil conflict of 1999, refusing evacuation while keeping up machine gun fire.
