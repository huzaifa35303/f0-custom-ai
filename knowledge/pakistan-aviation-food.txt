# Pakistan civil aviation airspace and food security
# One fact per line, phrased with the words someone would ask with.

Pakistan's airspace is managed by the Pakistan Civil Aviation Authority, PCAA, and serves as a transit corridor connecting Europe, the Middle East and East Asia.
The Karachi Flight Information Region, FIR, controls southern airspace over the Arabian Sea, Sindh and Balochistan, and interfaces with the Muscat and Mumbai FIRs.
The Lahore Flight Information Region, FIR, manages northern airspace over Punjab, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Gilgit-Baltistan and Azad Jammu and Kashmir, handing flights to Chinese and Afghan airspace.
Pakistan's en-route radar network uses primary surveillance radar, PSR, and monopulse secondary surveillance radar, MSSR, installed at Karachi, Pasni, Lahore and Islamabad.
ADS-B ground stations are deployed at high altitude sites such as Gilgit and Skardu to overcome terrain masking by the Karakoram and Hindu Kush ranges.

PASSCO, the Pakistan Agricultural Storage and Services Corporation, is the federal agency that maintains national strategic wheat reserves, buying from farmers at harvest and releasing grain during shortages.
Pakistan's concrete and steel grain silo complexes at Multan, Faisalabad and Hyderabad use hermetic sealing, temperature sensors and moisture control to store grain for up to 36 months without chemicals.
The strategic grain depots at Pipri and Landhi in coastal Karachi handle imported wheat during shortfalls and consolidate export surpluses for shipping.
Pakistan's inter-provincial supply corridors move wheat by rail and truck from surplus areas in southern Punjab and upper Sindh to deficit areas in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Balochistan and the cities.
