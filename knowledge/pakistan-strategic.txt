# Pakistan missile systems, mountain passes and nuclear installations
# One fact per line, phrased with the words someone would ask with.

Ghaznavi, also called Hatf-III, is a Pakistani short-range solid-fuel ballistic missile with a range of 290 km and a 700 kg payload.
Shaheen-II, also called Hatf-VI, is a Pakistani two-stage solid-propellant road-mobile medium-range ballistic missile with a range of 2,000 km and a 1,050 kg payload.
Ababeel is a Pakistani medium-range ballistic missile with a range of 2,200 km, carrying MIRV multiple independently targetable re-entry vehicles.
Babur-3 is Pakistan's submarine-launched cruise missile with a range of 450 km and a 450 kg payload, fired from Agosta-90B submarines to complete the sea-based second strike.

Khunjerab Pass is 4,693 metres high in the Karakoram range, links Gilgit-Baltistan to Xinjiang in China, and is the world's highest paved international border crossing and the main gate of CPEC.
Khyber Pass is 1,070 metres high in the Spin Ghar mountains and links Peshawar to Kabul in Afghanistan, historically the gateway for invasions of the subcontinent.
Bolan Pass is 1,793 metres high in the Toba Kakar range and links Quetta to Jacobabad and Sibi, connecting the Balochistan plateau to the Indus valley.
Lowari Pass is 3,118 metres high in the Hindu Kush and links Dir to Chitral; it was snow-blocked six months a year until the 10.4 km Lowari Tunnel opened.

Kahuta Research Laboratories, KRL, in Kahuta, Punjab, is Pakistan's gas centrifuge uranium enrichment facility.
The Khushab nuclear complex in Punjab is a heavy-water plutonium production site with several operational reactors.
Chagai-I and Chagai-II in the Ras Koh Hills and Kharan in Balochistan are Pakistan's underground nuclear test sites, where five simultaneous tests took place on 28 May 1998 and a further test on 30 May 1998.
